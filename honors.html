What?

I'm a Computer Science major with minors in Electrical Engineering and Mathematics, driven by a passion for intelligent systems and real-world problem-solving. My academic interests lie in artificial intelligence, computer vision, and embedded software systems, and I’ve pursued those passions through research roles at Texas A&M and beyond. These include my work at the Sketch Recognition Lab (SRL), contributions to the Institute for a Disaster Resilient Texas (IDRT), and most recently, a classified internship with the VICEROY MAVEN Program in partnership with AFRL.

Study abroad in Swansea, Wales, further expanded my worldview, exposing me to different teaching methods and cultural perspectives while continuing my research and coursework in AI. I’ve also been an active member of the Solar Car Racing Team, applying theory to engineering challenges in real-time.

So What?

These experiences have helped me grow not only as an engineer, but as a thinker. Working on diverse interdisciplinary teams has made me more reflective, communicative, and adaptable. Brain-Inspired AI and AI coursework abroad challenged me to consider different educational paradigms and perspectives on intelligence itself.

I’ve discovered that innovation is driven as much by curiosity as it is by practicality. Whether iterating on segmentation models or tuning generative pipelines for OSINT, I’ve found joy in building, learning, and problem-solving with intention. I now see myself not just as a builder, but as a researcher who questions how things work and why they matter.

Now What?

I plan to return to SRL to complete my undergraduate thesis on intelligent vision models, and finish my two research publications with AFRL. These research experiences will position me well to apply to competitive graduate schools like Stanford and UC Berkeley for a master's in Computer Science.

Long term, I aspire to work at the intersection of AI and automotive systems — applying intelligent software to embedded and real-time applications. My next step is to continue strengthening my technical foundations, deepening my research credentials, and preparing for the challenges and opportunities ahead. I hope to always stay curious, and always keep building.
