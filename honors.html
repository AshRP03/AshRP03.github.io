Honors Reflection

Connecting intellect, integrity, and impact through service and reflection.

What?

I'm a Computer Science major with minors in Electrical Engineering and Mathematics, drawn to AI, computer vision, and embedded systems. From research roles in the Sketch Recognition Lab to national security internships through the VICEROY MAVEN program, I've pursued opportunities where technology meets real-world challenge. I studied abroad in Wales, built solar cars, and presented research on disaster-resilient systems.

Through these experiences, I’ve developed technical depth and curiosity. I’m proud of projects in video segmentation, zero-trust inference, and intelligent systems for cyber-physical applications. I’ve taken on leadership in research, contributed to conference presentations, and grown across both hard and human-centered skills.

So What?

These moments weren’t just about skill—they changed how I think. Collaborating across disciplines has made me a more thoughtful builder and better listener. I’ve gained perspective, cultural awareness, and a deeper belief that good engineering is rooted in empathy.

I’ve learned that research isn't just technical—it's reflective. Whether solving image inference problems or mentoring incoming students, I’ve seen how impact comes from intention. These experiences remind me that growth is nonlinear, and value is found in the connections between systems, people, and purpose.

Now What?

My goal is to pursue a graduate degree in computer science at institutions like Stanford or Berkeley. I want to continue my work in intelligent vision systems and push boundaries in secure, trustworthy AI. As I return to SRL for my thesis and complete two AFRL publications, I’ll continue building the confidence and skill needed for that next step.

I also plan to stay grounded in service—through mentorship, outreach, and applying my skills to problems that matter. Whether through resilient infrastructure, future cars, or secure AI systems, I hope to help engineer a better world.

Mentorship & Service

As a Craig & Galen Brown Foundation Scholar, I’ve mentored incoming freshmen, helping them navigate academics, research, and college life. These conversations have grounded me, and taught me the value of listening and guidance.

My technical work also aligns with service—using AI for disaster resilience, autonomous response, and other public-good projects. I believe service includes any work that lifts others or strengthens the systems we all depend on.
